01

The problem

AI is power-hungry. It's mostly huge amounts of repetitive math, and every calculation uses electricity, which becomes heat.

On a phone that work has two outlets. It runs locally on processors that weren't built for it, which wastes energy, so you get heat and battery drain. Or it gets shipped to a data center, which costs money, time and privacy.

02

The NPU

Every phone chip has three processors: a that runs the phone, a for graphics, and an (neural processing unit) built for AI.

For AI, the NPU is the one that matters. AI is the same simple math repeated billions of times. The NPU is designed to do only that, so it uses a fraction of the power the CPU or GPU would. Less power means less heat and a longer battery. That's how AI can run on a phone all day.

HYPOTHESIS

The NPU's best work is (night-mode photos, live captions), so people credit the phone. Selling outcomes people feel, like battery, speed and privacy, may land better than specs.

06

Three customers

Qualcomm makes chips and sells to three kinds of customers.

  • (Samsung, Microsoft, Mercedes) buy the chips.
  • Developers build the apps and AI features that run on them.
  • Consumers never buy a chip directly but can ask for one by name, the way people once looked for "."
HYPOTHESIS

One truth, three messages: device makers buy performance, developers buy ease, consumers buy outcomes.

07

Three brands

Qualcomm sells its chips under three brands, one per market.

  • : phones, laptops, cars, glasses. Buyers want speed and battery life.
  • : robots, drones, factory equipment. Buyers want reliability for a decade.
  • : data centers. Buyers want lower cost and power use per answer.

Snapdragon for the device, Dragonfly for the cloud: Qualcomm's pitch for the . The thread Qualcomm sells across all three is decades of making chips that sip power.

HYPOTHESIS

Dragonwing and Dragonfly may be as much about reducing reliance on phones as about new markets.

08

The developer's problem

To use a chip's NPU, a developer has to adapt their AI model to that specific chip. Every chipmaker's NPU is different (Qualcomm, Apple, Google, MediaTek), so the work repeats for each one. Many developers skip it and use the cloud.

In data centers the lock-in is different: most AI software was written for , Nvidia's toolkit, which only works on Nvidia chips. Twenty years of habit make switching hard. That lock-in is called a .

HYPOTHESIS

Developers adopt whatever is easiest. Friction shows up in three numbers: time to first run, time to production quality, and whether they come back.

09

Qualcomm AI Stack

Qualcomm's set of that takes a developer's AI model and gets it running on Qualcomm's NPU.

The same tools work across Qualcomm's phones, PCs, cars and industrial chips, so developers learn them once.

10

Qualcomm AI Hub

A where developers grab AI models already shrunk and tuned for Qualcomm chips. There are 300+ to choose from, or they can upload their own. It's built on AI Stack, with the hard parts done for you.

Before shipping, they can test on 50+ real Qualcomm devices that Qualcomm hosts online, so nobody has to buy every phone.

HYPOTHESIS

AI Hub may be the most demo-friendly product in the portfolio, and one of the few places Qualcomm can see what developers actually do.

11

Modular

Qualcomm bought in 2026. Its software is built to let developers write AI code once and run it on many kinds of chips: Qualcomm's, Nvidia's and others.

Qualcomm's bet: if code runs anywhere, CUDA loses its grip, and customers choose chips on cost and power, where Qualcomm is strongest.

HYPOTHESIS

Level the field, then win on efficiency. It only works if developers trust a chipmaker to keep Modular neutral.

12

AI agents

A chatbot answers questions. An gets things done: "Move my 3pm, tell the team, and book dinner near the new office." It works across your apps, in many steps.

Agents need your personal data and work all day. Qualcomm's pitch: that's safer, faster and cheaper on your device than in the cloud.

HYPOTHESIS

Trust is the barrier. Running on the device doesn't automatically make an agent trusted.

THE TAKEAWAY

The right processor, the right mix of device and cloud, and tools that make it easy for developers.

Decades of design perfecting efficiency, keeping your devices cool and your costs low. From your pocket to the data center.
